# Helix DSP Ultra S — hardware-verified facts

Audiotec-Fischer Helix DSP Ultra S, 96 kHz, PC-Tool (v4/v5 era), measured on the Passat B8 rig
(`cars/vw-passat-b8-sedan-lhd`) with the **single-variable A/B protocol**: loopback sweep → change
ONE thing in the DSP, mic untouched → sweep; the complex ratio of the two sweeps is the device's
response to that change. Facts 1, 2, 3, 4, 5, 6, 8 and 9 use the **electrical** form of the same protocol — the processor's
output straight into the interface, no microphone and no cabin in the loop — which is the right shape
whenever the question is about the DSP's arithmetic rather than about a driver. Dates are when the
fact was pinned; "evidence" names what decided it.

| 1 | **AP1 and AP2 (the all-pass bands of the EQ bank) are bilinear-transformed DIGITAL biquads** — unity magnitude to 0.04 dB, phase `−2·atan(f/f0)` and `−2·atan2((f/f0)/Q, 1 − (f/f0)²)` only in the analogue limit. Model them the way fact 8 says to model the crossovers: at 4 kHz the two forms are 1.5° rms apart, at 8 kHz **3.6° rms / 9.5° max**, and the digital form fits to 0.1–0.3° rms. A free fit recovers what was entered to **0.05 % in f0 and 0.3 % in Q**. **Identifiability is set by the band together with the Q**, not by Q alone: after removing the best-fit delay and offset, an 8 kHz Q 0.7 section leaves 42.1°/17.5° over 1–20 kHz but only **0.8° over 2–8 kHz** — degenerate with delay + offset on a tweeter's band — while Q 4 still leaves 17.1° there. **The AP1 corner sits low, and the deviation grows with frequency** — four points now: −0.5 % at 250 Hz (248.8), −0.7 % at 1 kHz (993.2), −1.6 % at 4 kHz (3936) and **−4.9 % at 8 kHz (7611.6)**, while every AP2 corner lands within 0.05 % on the same rig in the same session. The shape stays a clean first-order all-pass — band-stable to ±0.3 % under four different fitting windows, magnitude flat to 0.02 dB — so it is the frequency that is wrong, not the form. **No simple law fits**: not a constant percentage, not a constant offset, and not the un-prewarped bilinear substitution, which would put 8 kHz at 7824. Modelling AP1 at the typed value costs 0.4° at 1 kHz, 0.9° at 4 kHz and **3.0° at 8 kHz** — and 8 kHz is where a tweeter all-pass lives. | `measurements/fact1-apf-{bypass,ap1-4k,ap2-4k-q0.7,ap2-8k-q0.7,ap2-8k-q4}.txt`, 2026-09-01, and `measurements/fact1-apf-s3-{bypass,ap1-250,ap1-1k,ap1-8k}.txt`, 2026-09-02, both **electrical**. Earlier, 2026-07, on a tweeter channel: a free fit recovered 4386 Hz / Q 3.82 against entered 4414 Hz / Q 4.0 with 31° RMS residual — consistent with this, and the acoustic path explains the residual. | verified |

| 3 | **Delays on the virtual-channel layer and the output layer SUM** (max 20.82 ms each) — enter a delay in one layer only. **A typed delay is then quantised to a whole sample**: 0.05 ms is 4.800 samples at 96 kHz and measures **5.0003**, 0.32 ms is 30.720 and measures **30.9996**, 1.00 ms is exactly 96 and measures 95.9981. One sample is **10.4167 µs**, and Audiotec-Fischer's published step of "3.5 mm or 0.01 ms" at 96 kHz is that figure rounded — **a model built on 0.01 ms drifts 4 %**, about 0.4 mm per 10 mm of claimed alignment. Both fractional values measured sat above the half-sample, so rounding and truncation upward are not separated by this data. | the summing: owner's PC-Tool practice, confirmed in the tune's audit trail. The quantisation: `measurements/delay-{0,0.05ms,0.32ms,1.00ms}.txt`, 2026-09-02, **electrical**, read off the phase slope of each ratio with 0.13° residual. | verified |

| 5 | **Bell (PK) bands are the RBJ peaking EQ, and the Q you type is the RBJ Q.** A free fit of the A/B ratio recovers the entered value: **Q 1 → 1.031**, **Q 4 → 4.028**, at 400 Hz and −12 dB. A "symmetric" definition would have shown up as a factor of two at this depth; it did not. **Boost mirrors cut**: +12 dB at Q 4 fits **Q 3.991 / +11.85 dB** against the cut's 4.028 / −11.95 dB — 0.9 % in Q and 0.10 dB in level. Re-measured electrically, with the cabin out of the loop, it is tighter and says more: at 1000 Hz a free fit returns the frequency to **0.03 %**, the Q to **1.1 %** and the gain to **0.03 dB**, leaving 0.012–0.05 dB rms. The half-gain edges land where RBJ puts them — **619.5 / 1610.6 Hz at Q 1** against 618.0 / 1618.0 predicted, **883.0 / 1132.4 at Q 4** against 882.8 / 1132.8 — the direct test that the typed Q is the half-gain Q and not a −3 dB bandwidth. **The width does not depend on the gain**: a +12 dB band read at its own half-gain gives the same edges as a +6 dB one. So the bank is **constant-Q**, not proportional-Q. | `measurements/fact5-bell-{cut-q1,cut-q4,boost-q4}-{before,after}.txt`, 2026-08-20: output layer, channel `w_L`, one band changed at a time, everything else cleared; fit residuals 0.97 / 0.56 / 0.19 dB. | 6 | **The channel Phase control is one RBJ second-order all-pass with Q = 1.0000**, whose corner the PC-Tool places so that the phase equals the setting **at the channel's reference crossover frequency**. The reference is that crossover **as entered** — the low-pass on a subwoofer channel, the high-pass on every other channel that has the control — and it does not care whether that filter is active, bypassed or set to `slope = OFF`: all three give the same reference, 0.018 dB and 0.12° apart. Reading the *active* crossover instead is the trap an implementation will fall into. **The corner is capped at about 18.0 kHz**, which is 3/16 of the 96 kHz rate, so every setting that would need a higher corner collapses onto that one filter: at a 5000 Hz reference the settings 5.625°, 11.25° and 28.125° are literally the same measurement and all deliver **29.5°**, a value not on the control's own grid. How many settings that costs at other crossovers is **arithmetic from the ceiling and the 5.625° step, not a separate measurement** — 2 lost at 2000 Hz, 3 at 3000, 5 at 5000, 9 at 8000, and the 1001 Hz boundary is where the first step needs exactly the ceiling. **Below a 1001 Hz reference nothing is lost** and the documented grid works exactly — one step measures −5.54° against a nominal 5.625°, two steps −11.29° against 11.25°. The ceiling is not documented by the manufacturer and this data cannot say whether it is deliberate or a defect. Q holds at 1.0000 across the reachable range (45° → 1.0000, 354.375° → 1.0001). **It is not a polarity flip**: 180° here is 180° only at the reference — at 1 kHz the same setting is −23° — and it costs group delay — computed from the fitted filter rather than read off the curve, 63 µs at 100 Hz rising to 130 µs at 5 kHz, with 270° worth about 0.1 ms in the bass. Per Audiotec-Fischer the control exists on subwoofer channels and on mid/high channels of a fully active system; `fullrange` and `low` channels get plain 0/180 polarity instead. | `measurements/phase-{ref,slopeoff,midhigh,steps,cap}-*.txt`, 2026-09-02, **electrical**, 37 sweeps in one session, each group against its own reference. Corners for a 180° setting: **2000.20 Hz** at a 2000 Hz reference against 2000.0 predicted, **4999.0** at 5000, **500.19** on a mid/high channel with the high-pass at 500 — that one from `ph180`/`ph0` alone, where the high-pass sits in both and cancels. The **500.47** for the same setting with the high-pass *bypassed* needs a third input, because that group has no bypassed reference: the high-pass leg is undone with the measured one from the cascade set, which is a cross-set operation and the only one in this file, **2998.2** at 3000. The ceiling recovered three times and agreeing within 3 Hz: 18010.2 Hz at a 5000 Hz reference, 18007.1 and 18009.3 at 3000. Fit residuals 0.10–0.29° rms, magnitude flat to 0.02 dB. Audiotec-Fischer's knowledge base (*Phase and time alignment*) documents the 5.625° grid, the 0–354.375° range and the reference rule, and says nothing about a ceiling. Reading and the author's response: DIMOSUS/Resonalyze#88. | verified |

| 8 | **The crossover alignments are textbook, and the implementation is a bilinear-transformed DIGITAL biquad chain — model it as such, not as an analogue prototype.** LR36 is a true 6th-order Linkwitz-Riley and BW42 a true 7th-order Butterworth, each landing on the frequency entered. At 1 kHz an analogue model and a digital one are indistinguishable (rms 0.114 vs 0.113 dB). At 8 kHz on this 96 kHz processor they are not: at the entered frequency the analogue model leaves **max 2.60 dB / rms 0.617 dB** where the bilinear one leaves **0.94 / 0.153**, four times better. Quote that pair rather than the detuning it implies — how far the analogue model has to be dragged to fit (−0.6 % on the published 1/96-octave curves, −1.2 % on the raw linear-spaced spectra) depends on how the fit weights the band, while the error at the nominal frequency does not. `tools/xover-model-check.py` prints both. A tweeter crossover modelled analytically will therefore not match this hardware. **The family closes at the low orders too**: LR12 and LR24 at 1 kHz land at **−6.01 / −6.05** and **−6.06 / −6.06 dB**, within 0.035–0.120 dB rms of theory and 0.4 % of the entered frequency, and the order beats its neighbours by 50–140× in rms when fitted freely. At 1 kHz these sets do not separate the analogue model from the digital one (0.02–0.05 dB apart) and are not meant to — they are the regression fixture; the 8 kHz sets are what decide the form. **Polarity follows the alignment**: LR36 needs one leg inverted (sum nulls by 37.5 dB without it, flat to 0.17 dB with), BW42 does not (flat to 0.17 dB either way, as odd-order Butterworth is). **And the chain simply multiplies** — a 500 Hz high-pass and a 5000 Hz low-pass measured separately and multiplied match the measurement of both engaged to **0.015 dB and 0.11° rms** on the published 1/96-octave files, over the 468 bins between 289 and 8426 Hz where both legs clear −20 dB (**0.045 dB / 0.30°** over the same window on REW's raw linear bins, which carry the un-averaged noise; 1.086 dB if the window is not masked at all, which is the stopband noise and not the cascade). Building a cascade as a product of sections is right on this hardware, and the corners hold when it is: 500.3 → 500.5 Hz and 5010.5 → 5001.4 Hz between alone and together. | `measurements/fact8-xover-{lr36-1k,bw42-1k,lr36-8k}-{bypass,lp,hp}.txt`, 2026-08-31, **electrical** — no microphone in the loop, so the cabin cannot contribute. Against theory over the band where each leg clears the chain's ≈ −50 dB floor: LR36 at 1 kHz within **0.36 dB / 6.1°** (LP) and **0.29 dB / 10.4°** (HP), −6.14 / −6.15 dB at crossover, LP−HP phase a constant **181.9° ± 2.3°**; BW42 within **0.84 dB / 2.8°** and **0.71 dB / 2.1°**, −3.08 / −3.11 dB at crossover, LP−HP **91.6° ± 6.0°**, slope −42.9 dB/oct. Order was fitted freely, not assumed: BW42 beats its neighbours by an order of magnitude (rms 0.171 dB against 1.399 for BW48 and 1.668 for BW36). `measurements/fact8-xover-lr{12,24}-1k-{lp,hp}.txt` + `fact8-xover-lr-1k-bypass.txt`, 2026-09-01, electrical. Sum control: **LR12 flat with one leg inverted** (max 0.10 dB, 46.9 dB null without it), **LR24 without** (0.09 dB, against 50.2 dB inverted). The cascade: `measurements/cascade-lr24-{500-hp,5000-lp,500hp-5000lp}.txt` over `measurements/level-gain0.txt`, 2026-09-02 — that set's own reference capture is disturbed and is published as `cascade-lr24-bypass-disturbed.txt` so that nobody uses it. | verified |
