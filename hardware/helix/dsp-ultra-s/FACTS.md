# Helix DSP Ultra S — hardware-verified facts

Audiotec-Fischer Helix DSP Ultra S, 96 kHz, PC-Tool (v4/v5 era), measured on the Passat B8 rig
(`cars/vw-passat-b8-sedan-lhd`) with the **single-variable A/B protocol**: loopback sweep → change
ONE thing in the DSP, mic untouched → sweep; the complex ratio of the two sweeps is the device's
response to that change. Fact 8 uses the **electrical** form of the same protocol — the processor's
output straight into the interface, no microphone and no cabin in the loop — which is the right shape
whenever the question is about the DSP's arithmetic rather than about a driver. Dates are when the
fact was pinned; "evidence" names what decided it.

| # | fact | evidence | status |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **AP1 and AP2 (the all-pass bands of the EQ bank) are bilinear-transformed DIGITAL biquads** — unity magnitude to 0.04 dB, phase `−2·atan(f/f0)` and `−2·atan2((f/f0)/Q, 1 − (f/f0)²)` only in the analogue limit. Model them the way fact 8 says to model the crossovers: at 4 kHz the two forms are 1.5° rms apart, at 8 kHz **3.6° rms / 9.5° max**, and the digital form fits to 0.1–0.3° rms. A free fit recovers what was entered to **0.05 % in f0 and 0.3 % in Q**. **Identifiability is set by the band together with the Q**, not by Q alone: after removing the best-fit delay and offset, an 8 kHz Q 0.7 section leaves 42.1°/17.5° over 1–20 kHz but only **0.8° over 2–8 kHz** — degenerate with delay + offset on a tweeter's band — while Q 4 still leaves 17.1° there. **The AP1 corner sits low**: −90° falls at **3936 Hz for an entered 4000 Hz** (−1.6 %), while both AP2 corners land within 0.05 %; the shape is right (0.12° residual with the frequency released), so it is an offset in the first-order section. One frequency only — not yet a law. | `measurements/fact1-apf-{bypass,ap1-4k,ap2-4k-q0.7,ap2-8k-q0.7,ap2-8k-q4}.txt`, 2026-09-01, **electrical**. Earlier, 2026-07, on a tweeter channel: a free fit recovered 4386 Hz / Q 3.82 against entered 4414 Hz / Q 4.0 with 31° RMS residual — consistent with this, and the acoustic path explains the residual. | verified |
| 2 | **LS_Q / HS_Q shelves are the RBJ shelf and the Q you type is the RBJ Q — across the whole range the processor allows, which is 0.3 … 2.** At Q 0.7071 this is the RBJ shelf with S = 1, as REW models it with its "LS Q"/"HS Q" types (REW's plain "Low shelf"/"High shelf" is a different definition). A free fit of the measured ratio returns **Q 0.300 / 0.707 / 1.998** for a low shelf and **0.300 / 0.707 / 2.001** for a high shelf, with the frequency to 0.2 %, the gain to 0.01 dB and 0.003–0.007 dB rms left over. **A model that ignores Q and builds the shelf at S = 1 is a different filter**, not an approximation: 1.86 dB rms and 4.33 dB max out at Q 2, 1.17 dB rms at Q 0.3 (100 Hz–10 kHz, +6 dB). | `measurements/fact2-shelfq-{bypass,ls-q0.3,ls-q0.7,ls-q2,hs-q0.3,hs-q0.7,hs-q2}.txt`, 2026-09-01, **electrical**, +6.0 dB at 1000 Hz, one band changed at a time. Consistent with REW's own Audiotec-Fischer equaliser export. | verified |
| 3 | **Delays on the virtual-channel layer and the output layer SUM** (max 20.82 ms each) — enter a delay in one layer only. | owner's PC-Tool practice, confirmed in the tune's audit trail. | verified in use |
| 4 | **30 PEQ bands per output channel; the channel gain is a separate control** (not in the EQ bank). The PC-Tool imports one channel's EQ from the tab-separated "Full EQ (30 bands)" bank — the same block REW exports with its Audiotec Fischer equaliser. | `formats/atf_full_eq_sample.txt` — a real REW export of a 20-band channel (PK bells + LS_Q/HS_Q shelves, 30 slots). | verified |
| 5 | **Bell (PK) bands are the RBJ peaking EQ, and the Q you type is the RBJ Q.** A free fit of the A/B ratio recovers the entered value: **Q 1 → 1.031**, **Q 4 → 4.028**, at 400 Hz and −12 dB. A "symmetric" definition would have shown up as a factor of two at this depth; it did not. **Boost mirrors cut**: +12 dB at Q 4 fits **Q 3.991 / +11.85 dB** against the cut's 4.028 / −11.95 dB — 0.9 % in Q and 0.10 dB in level. | `measurements/fact5-bell-{cut-q1,cut-q4,boost-q4}-{before,after}.txt`, 2026-08-20: output layer, channel `w_L`, one band changed at a time, everything else cleared; fit residuals 0.97 / 0.56 / 0.19 dB. Two unfiltered references 74 s apart agree to 0.16 dB RMS, which is this A/B's noise floor; references 13 minutes apart differ by 0.95 dB RMS, so each filter was ratioed against the reference nearest it in time. | verified |
| 7 | **A ±12 dB band at 400 Hz does not push this midbass out of linearity.** The boost fit returns +11.85 dB of the entered +12.00 with the smallest residual of the three (0.19 dB); compression or a rattle would have cost level and left structure in the residual. | the fact 5 boost pair. | verified for this driver, at this level, at this frequency |
| 8 | **The crossover alignments are textbook, and the implementation is a bilinear-transformed DIGITAL biquad chain — model it as such, not as an analogue prototype.** LR36 is a true 6th-order Linkwitz-Riley and BW42 a true 7th-order Butterworth, each landing on the frequency entered. At 1 kHz an analogue model and a digital one are indistinguishable (rms 0.114 vs 0.113 dB). At 8 kHz on this 96 kHz processor they are not: at the entered frequency the analogue model leaves **max 2.60 dB / rms 0.617 dB** where the bilinear one leaves **0.94 / 0.153**, four times better. Quote that pair rather than the detuning it implies — how far the analogue model has to be dragged to fit (−0.6 % on the published 1/96-octave curves, −1.2 % on the raw linear-spaced spectra) depends on how the fit weights the band, while the error at the nominal frequency does not. `tools/xover-model-check.py` prints both. A tweeter crossover modelled analytically will therefore not match this hardware. **The family closes at the low orders too**: LR12 and LR24 at 1 kHz land at **−6.01 / −6.05** and **−6.06 / −6.06 dB**, within 0.035–0.120 dB rms of theory and 0.4 % of the entered frequency, and the order beats its neighbours by 50–140× in rms when fitted freely. At 1 kHz these sets do not separate the analogue model from the digital one (0.02–0.05 dB apart) and are not meant to — they are the regression fixture; the 8 kHz sets are what decide the form. **Polarity follows the alignment**: LR36 needs one leg inverted (sum nulls by 37.5 dB without it, flat to 0.17 dB with), BW42 does not (flat to 0.17 dB either way, as odd-order Butterworth is). | `measurements/fact8-xover-{lr36-1k,bw42-1k,lr36-8k}-{bypass,lp,hp}.txt`, 2026-08-31, **electrical** — no microphone in the loop, so the cabin cannot contribute. Against theory over the band where each leg clears the chain's ≈ −50 dB floor: LR36 at 1 kHz within **0.36 dB / 6.1°** (LP) and **0.29 dB / 10.4°** (HP), −6.14 / −6.15 dB at crossover, LP−HP phase a constant **181.9° ± 2.3°**; BW42 within **0.84 dB / 2.8°** and **0.71 dB / 2.1°**, −3.08 / −3.11 dB at crossover, LP−HP **91.6° ± 6.0°**, slope −42.9 dB/oct. Order was fitted freely, not assumed: BW42 beats its neighbours by an order of magnitude (rms 0.171 dB against 1.399 for BW48 and 1.668 for BW36). `measurements/fact8-xover-lr{12,24}-1k-{lp,hp}.txt` + `fact8-xover-lr-1k-bypass.txt`, 2026-09-01, electrical. Sum control: **LR12 flat with one leg inverted** (max 0.10 dB, 46.9 dB null without it), **LR24 without** (0.09 dB, against 50.2 dB inverted). | verified |
| 9 | **The Bessel prototype is normalised at −3 dB, not for delay.** BE36 at 1000 Hz measures **−3.04 dB** (LP) and **−3.06 dB** (HP) at the corner, and sits within **0.034 / 0.036 dB rms** of the −3 dB-normalised prototype with the free corner at 1000.5 / 999.0 Hz. The delay-normalised prototype (unit group delay at DC) is only −0.40 dB at its own ω = 1 and needs a frequency scaling of 2.7034 to reach −3 dB, so the two conventions differ by 2.6 dB at the corner alone and by tens of dB further out. This is the normalisation TI SLOA049 tabulates and Resonalyze's `CrossoverFilter.BesselPrototype` uses. | `measurements/fact9-be36-1k-{bypass,lp,hp}.txt`, 2026-09-01, **electrical**. | verified |
